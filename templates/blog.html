Email

Cold emails that actually get replies

5 min read

Most cold emails fail before the second sentence. Here's the structure we see work again and again — and how to generate it in seconds with Pranox AI.

Read the full post

The biggest mistake in cold outreach isn't a weak subject line — it's writing for yourself instead of the reader. A good cold email answers one silent question in the first two sentences: "why is this relevant to me, right now?"

Structure that consistently performs well: open with a specific, researched detail about the recipient (not "I hope this finds you well"), state one clear value point, and close with a low-friction ask — a yes/no question, not a 30-minute call request.

Length matters more than people think. Once an email passes about 150 words, reply rates tend to drop, especially on mobile. Trim ruthlessly: every sentence should either build credibility or move toward the ask.

Resume

5 resume mistakes that cost you interviews

6 min read

Recruiters spend seconds, not minutes, on a first pass. These five fixable mistakes are the most common reason strong candidates get filtered out.

Read the full post

1. Listing duties instead of impact. "Responsible for managing social media" tells a recruiter what you were assigned, not what changed because of you. Reframe around outcomes: what grew, shrank, sped up, or got fixed.

2. No numbers anywhere. Even rough, honest estimates ("reduced response time by roughly 30%") are far more convincing than adjectives like "significant" or "major".

3. One resume for every job. The same resume sent to ten different roles reads as generic to all ten. Mirror the language of the job description where it's genuinely true of your experience.

4. Burying the most relevant experience. Recruiters often read top-down and stop early. Your strongest, most relevant point should never be three jobs down the page.

5. Skipping the summary. A two-line summary at the top, naming your target role and your strongest proof point, gives a skimming reader the headline before they decide whether to keep reading.

What Deep Research is, and when to reach for it

4 min read

AI Chat is great for a quick answer. Deep Research exists for a different kind of question — one that needs more than one source to answer properly.

Read the full post

A single chat response is built to answer a focused question quickly. But some questions aren't really single-answer questions — "compare these three approaches," "what's changed in this space recently," "summarize the current state of X" — these need information pulled together from multiple places and reconciled into one coherent answer.

That's the gap Deep Research fills. Instead of one generated response, it gathers from multiple sources and synthesizes them into a structured summary, so you're not the one cross-referencing five tabs.

Good candidates for Deep Research: market or competitor comparisons, "what's the current best practice for…", literature-style summaries, and topics where a single, possibly outdated answer isn't good enough on its own.

Stick with AI Chat for direct questions, code help, drafting, and brainstorming — it's faster and just as accurate for those.
